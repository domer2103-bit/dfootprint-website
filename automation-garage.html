Introducing Due Date

Every unanswered call is a car going to the garage down the road.

Due Date is five small automations that quietly keep your diary full — without you doing anything different.

01/05 Missed Call Handler

The phone rings while you're under a car.

By the time you're free to call back, they've already rung the next garage on Google. You never even find out you lost the job.

An automatic text goes out the second you miss a call, asking what they need and their reg — so when you do call back, you're calling with the full picture instead of guessing who it was and why.

02/05 Day-Before Reminder

A booked slot with nobody showing up is an hour of paid labour standing empty.

Every customer gets a text the day before their appointment, with an easy way to reschedule if they can't make it — so your diary reflects who's actually coming in, not just who booked three weeks ago and forgot.

03/05 Review Requests & No-Show Recovery

Great work doesn't show up in your Google reviews unless someone remembers to ask.

And most people forget the moment they drive off. The day after a job, everyone who showed up gets asked for a quick review automatically. Everyone who didn't gets a friendly nudge to rebook — no awkward in-person ask required either way.

04/05 Annual MOT Renewal Reminder

An MOT that lapses without warning is a customer who books wherever's convenient that week.

Not necessarily you. Two automatic reminders land before every customer's MOT is due — a fortnight out, then again three days out if they still haven't booked — so renewals come back to you by default, not by luck.

05/05 Bonus Broadcast

Sometimes you just want to send everyone a message.

A seasonal offer, a thank you, a heads up you're closed for a few days. One click sends it to your whole customer list, whenever you actually have something worth saying — nothing scheduled, nothing automatic, entirely up to you.

What this won't do

It won't fix a bad first impression on the phone, and it won't bring in customers who were never going to choose you. It just makes sure the ones who do call — or who've already trusted you once — don't quietly slip through the cracks.
