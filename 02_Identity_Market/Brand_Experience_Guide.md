# Brand Experience Guide

> **Layer:** Identity & Market | **Purpose:** The emotional and communication experience people should have interacting with Digital Footprint, across every channel | **Status:** Active — source of truth | **Owner:** Founder | **Version:** 1.0

No marketing copy or slogans here. `writing-style.md` already defines tone, voice, and format rules; `brand-guidelines.md` already defines positioning and personality. This document sits above both — it defines the feeling the brand should produce in a real person at each stage of contact, and the specific behaviours (and anti-behaviours) that produce or destroy that feeling, regardless of which channel is being used.

## 1. Desired Client Feeling

**Before discovering Digital Footprint:** the visitor should arrive already carrying some frustration or curiosity (per `Website_Strategy.md` section 2). Nothing to design for here except making sure the first contact meets that feeling with recognition, not a pitch — the goal on first contact is for them to feel "this person actually gets it," not "this person wants something from me."

**During the Assessment:** calm, respected, and genuinely listened to — not interrogated, not sold to, not rushed. A client should feel comfortable admitting something isn't working, without embarrassment, because the process has already told them (per `Assessment_Interview_Method.md` section 1) that this is normal and expected. By the end of the session, the feeling should be relief at having been heard properly, even before any answer has been given.

**After receiving recommendations:** clarity, not persuasion — the client should feel like they understand their own business slightly better than they did before, regardless of what was recommended. If the outcome is a build, the feeling should be confidence in a well-reasoned next step, not excitement manufactured by a sales pitch. If the outcome is "no action" or "not AI," the feeling should be reassurance and validation — that paying for this was still worth it, because now they actually know, rather than continuing to guess.

## 2. Communication Principles

**How Digital Footprint explains complex topics:** always translated into the client's own business terms before any technical or AI-specific language is used, if it's used at all — per `writing-style.md`'s standard of explaining hard things simply. A complex idea explained well should make the client feel smarter, never smaller.

**How it talks about AI:** as a tool with real limits, not a force with unlimited potential — described in terms of what specific task it's suited to and why, never in sweeping or abstract terms ("AI will change everything about how you work"). AI is mentioned only after the actual business problem has been established, never before, mirroring the ordering already built into `Client_Intake_Form.md` and `Founder_Outreach_System.md`.

**How it communicates uncertainty:** openly and specifically, not hidden behind confident-sounding hedges. "I don't know yet, and I'd rather find out properly than guess" (per `Founder_Outreach_System.md` section 7) is the model — uncertainty is stated as a reason for rigor, not as a weakness to be talked around.

**How it communicates "no AI needed":** with the same weight, structure, and confidence as any positive recommendation (per `Prescription_Decision_Framework.md` section 6) — never softened, apologised for, or buried. Framed explicitly, where relevant, as evidence the process is working correctly, not as a disappointing result.

## 3. Tone Guidelines

**What we sound like:** a knowledgeable, honest, slightly understated person who'd rather under-claim and over-deliver than the reverse. Calm and unhurried, even when explaining something the client is anxious about. Warm without being falsely enthusiastic — someone who takes the client's business as seriously as the client does.

**What we avoid sounding like:** a hype-driven tech salesperson. A corporate agency speaking in generic, interchangeable business language. An overly casual or jokey voice that undermines being taken seriously on something the client is genuinely worried about. An intimidating technical expert who makes the client feel behind or foolish for not already knowing something.

(Full tone/voice/formatting mechanics — sentence length, formality, emoji use — remain defined in `writing-style.md`; this section is about emotional register, not mechanics.)

## 4. Trust Signals

**Honesty:** stating plainly when something isn't known yet, when a recommendation is "no," or when a previous approach (a client's own prior fix attempt, or Digital Footprint's own past pilot mistake) didn't work. Publishing real "no action" outcomes as visibly as build outcomes once case studies exist (per `Website_Strategy.md` section 4).

**Expertise:** demonstrated through the quality of questions asked, not through jargon volume or credential-listing. Giving away real thinking for free (`Market_Entry_Plan.md` section 3) is itself a trust signal — expertise that's confident enough to be shared openly, rather than gatekept behind a sales call.

**Calm confidence:** never rushing a client toward a decision, never filling silence with persuasion, being comfortable saying "let's not do anything yet" as easily as "let's build this." Confidence shows in what's declined, not just in what's offered.

**Practical thinking:** recommendations sized to the client's actual situation, not the biggest possible opportunity (`AI Prescription.md` section 4) — a client should notice that suggestions feel proportionate and realistic, not aspirational or oversized for a business their size.

## 5. Things That Would Damage Trust

**AI hype** — language implying AI is transformative, magical, or inevitable rather than a specific tool for a specific job; per `Company Philosophy.md` Principle 6 and the phrases explicitly banned in `Website_Strategy.md` section 4.

**Exaggerated promises** — any claim of time or money saved not backed by conservative, real evidence (`ROI_Calculator.md`); any implied guarantee that an engagement will lead to a build.

**Unnecessary complexity** — jargon used to sound expert rather than to be precise, over-engineered recommendations where a simpler fix would do (`Company Philosophy.md` Principle 10, `coding-principles.md`), or a report so dense the client can't act on it.

**Selling before understanding** — proposing a specific product, tool, or fix before the diagnosis is complete; treating a client's own request as a scoped brief instead of a hypothesis (`AI Prescription.md` v1.1); using outreach or marketing language that implies a specific solution before any conversation has happened (`Founder_Outreach_System.md` section 4).

Any one of these, even in a single email or a single social post, does more damage than several good interactions can repair — trust here is asymmetric, and should be treated that way in every channel decision.

## 6. Consistency Rules

**Website:** every page follows `Website_Strategy.md` and `Brand_Experience_Guide.md` sections 2-5 — no page may use hype language, exaggerated claims, or a sales-before-understanding structure, even on pages with strong commercial intent like the Assessment page.

**Emails:** same voice as the website and the founder's direct speech — no switching into a more "salesy" register just because email is a common place for pitch-style writing elsewhere. Uncertainty and "no" outcomes communicated with the same weight as in a live conversation, per section 2.

**Assessment reports:** the highest-stakes consistency point — `Prescription_Report_Template.md` and `Prescription_Decision_Framework.md` section 5-6 already encode this experience directly into the deliverable itself; this guide's principles are not new instructions for the report, they're the reason those instructions exist the way they do.

**Social media:** founder-voice only, per `Market_Entry_Plan.md` section 4 — no separate, more polished or corporate "brand voice" allowed to develop here that diverges from how the founder actually talks to a client one-to-one. Content topics and avoided topics per `Market_Entry_Plan.md` section 3 apply directly.

**Client conversations:** the least scripted and most important channel — `Assessment_Interview_Method.md` and `Founder_Outreach_System.md` already define behaviour here in detail; this guide's job is making sure the same person shows up whether they're writing a webpage or sitting across from a client, not two different personas depending on the channel.

## Underlying Standard

Every channel, every piece of communication, checkable against the same three lines already anchoring the rest of the system: diagnose before prescribe, trust before technology, clarity before automation. If a channel-specific convention (a marketing best practice, an email growth tactic, a social media trend) would require breaking one of these to work, it doesn't get adopted, regardless of how normal it is elsewhere.
