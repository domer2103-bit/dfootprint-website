# Pricing Strategy

> **Layer:** Identity & Market | **Purpose:** How Digital Footprint thinks about pricing decisions over time — not a price list, not marketing copy | **Status:** Active — source of truth | **Owner:** Founder | **Version:** 1.0

Built from the Decision Framework analysis run for the founding-client price decision (per `decision_framework.md`). This document is the reasoning that decision came from, kept as a standing reference so future pricing decisions follow the same discipline rather than being made ad hoc.

## 1. Pricing Philosophy

Digital Footprint charges for the diagnosis itself, independent of what it recommends, because the diagnosis is the actual product — not a preview of one. This follows directly from `AI Prescription.md` section 2: "we do not diagnose problems in order to sell solutions, we sell diagnosis because clarity has value, and solutions should come after understanding." A free diagnosis would quietly turn the Assessment into a sales funnel, which contradicts the entire "diagnose before prescribe" foundation the business is built on (`00 Vision.md`, `Company Philosophy.md` Principle 3).

Charging real money also protects the honesty of the process on both sides. A client who has paid something real treats the engagement seriously rather than as a casual favour, and a genuine invoice reduces the temptation, on the consultant's side, to treat the diagnosis as disposable. Price is not set to maximise how many people say yes — it's set to make a yes mean something.

## 2. Current Founding Client Offer

**Product:** AI Prescription Assessment.

**Founding client price:** £197.

**Availability:** limited to the first 5 clients.

**Purpose of the offer:** to validate genuine willingness to pay from real, unproven-relationship businesses, without a barrier so high it stalls recruitment inside the 30-day pilot window defined in `10 Roadmap.md` Phase 1. The price is deliberately close to what a UK small business owner already associates with a private specialist consultation, giving it a real, understood anchor rather than an arbitrary number.

**What is included:** the full Assessment as specified in `AI_Prescription_Assessment_Specification.md` — Business Health Check, diagnosis session, full analysis against `Prescription_Decision_Framework.md`, a written Prescription Report covering every friction point investigated, and a debrief call. No implementation, build, or ongoing support is included at this price — those are always a separate, subsequently scoped engagement.

## 3. Future Pricing Direction

**Expected future range:** £350-£600.

**Why pricing can increase after validation:** the founding-client rate exists specifically because Digital Footprint currently has no case studies, no track record, and no proof the diagnosis reliably lands well with strangers. Once that evidence exists, the price should reflect the reduced risk a new client is taking on by hiring an now-proven process, not an unproven one. This is a standard, honest reason for a price increase — not opportunism.

**What evidence is required before changing price:** at minimum, the completion of the 5 founding-client pilots and the Phase 1 review point defined in `10 Roadmap.md`. Evidence required specifically for a pricing decision (not just a methodology decision): a genuine read on willingness to pay at £197 (did clients pay without heavy negotiation), at least one usable, honestly-told case study or anonymised outcome, and a stable, repeatable delivery process (per `11 SOP.md`) that hasn't required significant rework since the founding cohort. Price should not move up simply because five engagements are complete if the evidence above doesn't support it yet.

## 4. Pricing Rules

**Never use fake scarcity.** The "first 5 clients" limit must be literally true. If demand exceeds five before the founding cohort is full, the offer closes at five and any further interest is quoted the future range — not a flexible, marketing-driven "limited spots" claim, per `Brand_Experience_Guide.md` section 5 and `Website_Strategy.md` section 4.

**Never extend founding pricing dishonestly.** Once five founding clients are secured, the founding rate ends. A sixth or seventh interested client is not quietly given the same rate to close the sale — doing so would make every future claim about limited offers meaningless, and would itself be a small, self-inflicted violation of `Company Philosophy.md` Principle 6 ("no hype, ever").

**Never reduce price simply because recruitment is slow.** If the 30-day window passes without five paying clients at £197, the correct response is to extend the timeline, reconsider outreach approach (`Founder_Outreach_System.md`), or examine why interest is lower than expected — not to discount further under time pressure. This is optimising for the wrong thing (client count) at the expense of the actual validation goal (genuine willingness to pay), per `Company Philosophy.md` Principle 7 ("client fit over client count") and Principle 9 ("progress over perfection, never rushed").

**Never allow price to influence diagnosis outcome.** A client having paid £197 must never create pressure to recommend a build in order to feel the fee was "earned." The anti-sales checks in `Prescription_Decision_Framework.md` section 4 apply with full force regardless of price paid — if anything, they matter more once real money is involved, not less.

## 5. Validation Signals

Tracked in `Validation_Dashboard.md` and `Pilot_Execution_Dashboard.md`, with pricing-specific attention to:

**Willingness to pay** — did the client pay £197 without heavy negotiation or hesitation; how the price was reacted to when first stated, not just whether payment eventually happened.

**Reaction to "no AI needed"** — whether a client who paid and received a "do nothing" or "not AI" recommendation still reports the Assessment felt worthwhile, per `AI Prescription.md` section 7's Level 2 (Human Impact) measurement. This is the single most important signal for whether £197 is priced correctly relative to what's actually delivered.

**Perceived value of the report** — asked directly at debrief, independent of outcome: did the written report feel like it was worth what was paid for it.

**Implementation interest after Assessment** — whether clients who received a build recommendation express genuine interest in proceeding, and at what price sensitivity — early signal for whether the future £350-£600 range, and eventual implementation pricing, is realistic.

## 6. Review Process

Pricing is reviewed at the Phase 1 review point defined in `10 Roadmap.md` (after 5 completed pilots or 30 days, whichever comes first), not before, and not ad hoc mid-pilot — consistent with Governance.md's "no changes from single feedback, repeated patterns create improvements" rule. A pricing change is a promotion candidate like any other finding: it requires the evidence outlined in section 3, reviewed deliberately, and logged with reasoning in this document's changelog once decided, not changed silently.

Between review points, if a single client's reaction to price (positive or negative) feels significant, it is logged in `13 Lessons Learned.md` as a one-off observation and watched, per `Post_Conversation_Review_Process.md` — not acted on immediately, regardless of how compelling it seems in the moment.
