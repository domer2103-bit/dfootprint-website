# Market Entry Plan

> **Layer:** Operations (working document, not source of truth) | **Purpose:** How Digital Footprint enters the market while staying aligned with its philosophy | **Status:** Active | **Owner:** Founder | **Version:** 1.0

No new methodology or consulting framework here — this applies what already exists (`Company Philosophy.md`, `brand-guidelines.md`, `AI Prescription.md`, `Founder_Outreach_System.md`) to the specific question of how Digital Footprint becomes visible and reachable, without contradicting anything already built.

## 1. Positioning

**How Digital Footprint should explain itself:** a diagnosis-first practice for small and medium businesses — "I diagnose where your business is losing time, and prescribe the simplest fix, which is sometimes AI and sometimes isn't," per the "AI Prescription Guy" positioning already established in `brand-guidelines.md`. Every explanation of the business should be able to be said in one sentence, in plain language, without naming a single technology first.

**What it is not:** not an AI agency that happens to sell AI. Not a reseller of a specific tool or platform. Not a company whose default answer is "yes, AI can do that" — the explicit, repeatable existence of "no" as an outcome is the differentiator, not a caveat to downplay. Not a company chasing whatever AI trend is currently in the news (`Company Philosophy.md` Principle 6, "no hype ever").

**Why diagnosis comes before implementation:** stated plainly wherever the business is described — because a solution applied before the real problem is understood usually makes the underlying confusion faster, not better (`AI Prescription.md` section 1's foundational hypothesis-vs-reality distinction). This isn't a sales technique dressed as philosophy; it's the actual reason the Assessment product exists as a standalone, paid thing.

## 2. Website Requirements

Kept minimal and honest — no page should claim more than the business can currently back up with real evidence. Full page-level detail (visitor mindset, trust problem solved, desired next action, conversion journey, and future scalability) is defined in `Website_Strategy.md` — the summary below is a brief overview, not the canonical version.

**Home** — states the one-sentence positioning above, and the core promise (diagnosis before prescription, no-AI is a valid outcome), with a single clear next step (book an Assessment or read about the approach). No client logos, no fabricated stats, no "trusted by" claims until genuinely true.

**About / The Founder** — drawn from `about-founder.md`: background, why this business exists, told honestly, including the non-technical starting point — this is a credibility asset with the target client, not something to hide.

**The AI Prescription (Methodology)** — a plain-language explanation of the diagnose-before-prescribe approach, closely following `AI Prescription.md`'s core sequence but written for a business owner, not a consultant. This page does the actual educating (see section 3) and should be able to stand alone as useful even to someone who never books an Assessment.

**The Assessment** — what it is, who it's for, what it costs, what happens at each stage, and — stated plainly, not buried — that the outcome might be "you don't need this," per `AI_Prescription_Assessment_Specification.md`. This is the direct commercial page, and it should read as the most honest page on the site, not the most persuasive one.

**Case Studies / Results** — left genuinely empty or clearly marked "coming soon" until real pilot outcomes exist to publish, per `Founder_Outreach_System.md`'s honesty standard about being early. Never filled with invented or composite examples presented as real.

**Contact / Book** — a simple way to start a conversation, framed as the outreach opening in `Founder_Outreach_System.md` section 3, not a hard sales form.

**Core message across every page:** trust before technology. If a sentence on the site could be said by any generic AI agency, it's probably wrong for this site.

## 3. Content Strategy

**Topics that demonstrate expertise:** what the five AI-suitability rejection conditions actually mean for a business owner, in plain terms. How to tell if a business problem is really a process problem wearing a technology costume (drawing on real patterns like the Marlow & Co./GreenEdge repeated-fix pattern from the stress tests, anonymised). What a good diagnosis actually looks like, and why it's worth paying for independently of any build. Honest explainers of what AI is currently good and bad at, without hype in either direction. Real anonymised case summaries once pilots produce them, including "no action" outcomes presented with the same confidence as build outcomes, per `Prescription_Decision_Framework.md` section 6.

**Topics to avoid:** generic "10 AI tools every business needs" listicles — this is exactly the noise the whole positioning exists to cut through. Reactive commentary on whatever AI news broke this week, which signals hype-chasing rather than the "no hype ever" principle. Deep technical AI content aimed at other AI professionals rather than the actual target client. Anything that implies AI is always the answer, even subtly, through tone or repeated topic choice.

**How to educate without selling:** give away the actual thinking, not just teasers gated behind a sales call — explain the diagnostic questions and the suitability conditions openly, since understanding them doesn't replace the value of having someone apply them properly to your specific business. This mirrors the "teach as much as build" instinct already established in `00 Vision.md` — content itself is a small-scale version of that same teaching mission, not a funnel dressed up as education.

## 4. Lead Generation Channels

**Local businesses:** direct outreach per `Founder_Outreach_System.md`, drawing on existing local connections (including the founder's own trade/CNC industry network) as a starting point for warm introductions, expanding to semi-cold local contact matching `ideal_client.md`.

**LinkedIn:** founder-voice content only, not a branded company presence pretending to be bigger than it is — posts built from the content strategy above (section 3), written in the founder's own voice per `writing-style.md`, including honest posts about the pilot process itself as it happens, since transparency about being early is itself credible content.

**Partnerships:** accountants, bookkeepers, and local business advisors are natural referral partners — they routinely see business friction up close but don't fix it themselves, and a diagnosis-first offer is easy for them to recommend without reputational risk, since it doesn't commit their client to buying anything. Approached honestly, the same way as any other outreach conversation, not as a formal channel partnership before any relationship exists.

**Networking:** local business meetups, breakfast networking groups, or trade association events reachable given the founder's limited weekly hours — chosen for quality of attendee fit over frequency of events, consistent with the 2-3 hour/day capacity constraint already established.

## 5. First 90-Day Objectives

**Days 1-30:** complete the pilot phase exactly as defined in `10 Roadmap.md` Phase 1 and `AI_Prescription_Pilot_Playbook.md` — 5 real Assessments, Phase 1 review point reached. No public-facing market entry activity (website, content, broad outreach) competes for time during this window; the pilot takes priority.

**Days 31-60:** build the minimal website (section 2) using real material from the completed pilots wherever possible — at minimum, honestly worded case summaries even if anonymised or thin. Begin light LinkedIn presence, 1-2 posts a week, drawn from real pilot learning rather than generic content. Approach 1-2 potential referral partners (accountants/advisors) from existing local contacts.

**Days 61-90:** assess whether pilot-sourced leads plus early content/partnership activity are producing new qualifying conversations without paid advertising. Metrics to observe: number of inbound conversations sourced from the website or LinkedIn (versus direct outreach), number of referral partner introductions received, whether "no action" pilot outcomes are being referenced positively in any conversation as a trust signal, and whether the 2-3 hour/day capacity is being consumed sustainably by this combined workload (pilots plus market entry) or needs re-prioritising.

**Realistic founder goals for the 90 days overall:** a working, honest website; a small, real body of case material; the beginning of a referral relationship or two; and clear evidence of which lead generation channel (if any) is worth investing further time in — not a large client base, and not yet a stable pipeline.

## 6. Risks

**Becoming another AI agency.** Mitigated by keeping every public-facing page and piece of content anchored to the diagnosis-first sequence rather than a technology list, and by publishing "no action" or "no AI" outcomes as visibly and proudly as build outcomes once real ones exist — the "no" rate is proof of independence (`AI_Prescription_Assessment_Product.md` section 7), and hiding it in marketing would undercut the entire differentiator.

**Overpromising.** Mitigated by the same conservative ROI standard already built into `ROI_Calculator.md` and `Prescription_Decision_Framework.md` — nothing on the website or in content should claim outcomes beyond what real pilot evidence actually supports, and no fabricated statistics or composite success stories should ever substitute for genuinely thin early evidence.

**Attracting the wrong clients.** Mitigated by keeping the qualification discipline from `Founder_Outreach_System.md` section 4 applied consistently even as inbound leads (from the website or content) start to arrive, rather than relaxing it under pressure to fill the pipeline — an inbound lead deserves the same fit-check as a cold outreach conversation, not an easier pass because they came looking.

## Underlying Standard

Every choice in this plan should be checkable against the same three lines the whole system has been built around: diagnose before prescribe, no AI needed is a valid outcome, trust before technology. If a marketing or growth tactic would only work by quietly bending one of these, it doesn't belong in this plan, regardless of how effective it might be in the short term.
