# AI Prescription — Pilot Playbook

> **Layer:** Operations (working document, not source of truth) | **Purpose:** Practical founder operating guide for running the first five real AI Prescription Assessments | **Status:** Active | **Owner:** Founder | **Version:** 1.0

This is execution, not methodology — every rule referenced here already exists in `03_Knowledge_Methodology/` and `04_Delivery/`. This document doesn't redefine any of it; it sequences it into a single runnable plan for the founder, alone, running the first five pilots. Where a decision or standard already has a source-of-truth document, this playbook points to it rather than restating it.

## 1. Pilot Objective

**What we are trying to validate:** whether the diagnosis-first Assessment creates genuine value for real business owners — not whether they'll buy an AI build. Specifically: does a stranger pay for it, complete the process, and react to an honest "no" the same way they'd react to a "yes."

**What success means:** at least a majority of the 5 pilots pay without heavy negotiation, complete the full process, and report the diagnosis itself was valuable — regardless of what it recommended. One or more "no action" or "not AI" outcomes landing well with the client is as strong a success signal as an implementation sale.

**What we are deliberately not trying to prove yet:** that Digital Footprint can convert diagnoses into implementation revenue at scale, that the methodology works identically across every industry, or that this is a repeatable business at volume. This pilot tests the front half of the business (does the diagnosis land), not the back half (does implementation follow reliably).

## 2. Ideal Pilot Client Criteria

**Who should participate:** roughly matches `ideal_client.md` — 5-50 employees, owner still hands-on daily, no IT department — sourced as a deliberate mix (2 from personal network, 3 cold/semi-cold), per `10 Roadmap.md` Phase 1.

**Who should be rejected:** businesses clearly outside the target profile (very large, fully corporate, or a one-person operation with no team dynamics to diagnose), anyone who wants to skip the diagnosis and go straight to "just build me X," and close friends or family whose "yes" would be social rather than genuine.

**Why the wrong client creates bad learning:** a mismatched client either produces a diagnosis that doesn't test the real target market, or produces a false-positive "yes" that isn't a genuine signal — either way, it costs one of only five learning opportunities in this pilot without paying back real information. Better to have four real pilots than five, one of which was the wrong client.

## 3. Client Acquisition Process

**How potential pilot clients are identified:** direct, personal outreach — existing network first, then local/semi-cold contacts matching the criteria above. No paid advertising or broad marketing during this phase; the pilot needs quality conversations, not volume.

**How the first conversation works:** lead with the diagnosis framing directly, not a pitch — state plainly what's being offered (a paid, reduced-rate "founding client" diagnosis) and that the honest outcome might be "you don't need this." This mirrors the opening already defined in `AI_Prescription_Client_Experience.md` section 2, just moved earlier, into the sales conversation itself, not just the engagement.

**How diagnosis-first positioning is explained:** "I help small businesses figure out where they're losing time to repetitive work, and whether AI is actually worth using to fix it — sometimes the answer is no. That's not a caveat, that's the actual point." Said plainly, not as a disclaimer buried at the end.

**How to avoid sounding like a salesperson:** ask more than you tell in the first conversation. Don't describe AI capabilities or Automation Catalog products at this stage — that's diagnosis work, not qualification work, and jumping to it early undermines the entire "diagnose before prescribe" positioning before the paid engagement has even started.

## 4. Qualification Process

**Questions before accepting a client:** roughly how many people work there, is the owner still hands-on day to day, is there anything repetitive or time-consuming that's been bothering them, would they be open to paying a reduced fee as an early client. (Same pre-qualifying questions as `10 Roadmap.md` Phase 1.)

**Fit criteria:** matches `ideal_client.md` reasonably well, genuinely curious rather than skeptical about the process, willing to commit real (even if reduced) payment.

**Warning signs:** insists on a specific solution before any diagnosis happens, treats the reduced pilot price as leverage to negotiate further, can't articulate any real friction when asked directly, or is a close personal relationship where a "no" from you would damage the relationship rather than just end an engagement.

## 5. Assessment Delivery Workflow

| Stage | Purpose | Owner | Inputs | Outputs | Approx. Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lead | Identify a possible pilot client | Founder | Personal network / cold outreach | A named prospect | Ongoing |
| Initial conversation | Qualify fit, explain diagnosis-first framing | Founder | Qualification questions (section 4) | Go/no-go decision | 10-15 min |
| Assessment agreement | Confirm scope, price, and expectations before starting | Founder | Section 2 of `AI_Prescription_Client_Experience.md` | Signed agreement, payment | 15-20 min |
| Client Intake Form | Create the initial hypothesis | Client (self-serve) | `Client_Intake_Form.md` v2.0 | Completed intake answers | Client's own time, target 3 days |
| Preparation Brief | Prepare the consultant without concluding | Founder (with AI assistance per the design, not yet built) | Completed intake | `Client_Preparation_Brief.md`, reviewed and signed off | 30-45 min |
| Assessment Interview | Discover reality | Founder | Preparation Brief | Session notes per `Client_Conversation_Capture_Template.md` | 60-90 min |
| Analysis | Apply Decision Framework to every friction point | Founder | Session notes | Per-friction-point outcomes | 60-90 min |
| Prescription Report | Deliver findings and recommendations | Founder | Analysis output | Completed `Prescription_Report_Template.md` | 60-90 min |
| Debrief | Explain findings, check understanding | Founder | Finished report | Client understanding, decision | 20-30 min |
| Implementation / Referral / No Action | Client decides next step | Client | Debrief outcome | Engagement continues, ends, or is referred | Client's own timeline |

Total founder time per pilot: roughly 4-6 hours across the paid stages, consistent with a 2-3 hour/day working budget spread over the week each pilot runs.

## 6. Founder Preparation Checklist

Before the first client: pricing decided for the "founding client" reduced rate. Payment method ready (invoicing or online payment). A blank copy of every template (`Client_Intake_Form.md`, `Client_Preparation_Brief.md`, `Prescription_Report_Template.md`) saved and ready to duplicate per client. A blank `Client_Template.md` record ready in `05_Client_Records/` for the first real client. `Validation_Dashboard.md` open and ready to update after every stage. The opening lines for the first conversation (section 3) rehearsed enough to say naturally, not read off a script.

## 7. Client Communication Standards

**Before assessment:** plain, upfront statement of what's being bought (diagnosis, not a guaranteed build), that "no AI needed" is a legitimate outcome, and what happens at each stage and roughly when.

**During assessment:** per `Assessment_Interview_Method.md` — curious, evidence-seeking, no conclusions offered live, no reassurance about likely findings.

**When challenging assumptions:** respectfully, using the client's own words, asking permission before pressing on something sensitive — per `Assessment_Interview_Method.md` section 5.

**When recommending no AI:** stated as plainly and confidently as any "yes," paired with a concrete alternative, never hedged or apologised for — per `Prescription_Decision_Framework.md` section 6 and `AI_Prescription_Client_Experience.md` section 7.

**When recommending no further action:** the same standard — evidence-led, confident, paired with a concrete, stated condition for revisiting, per Case 5 in `Full_System_StressTest.md` as the reference example of what this should sound like.

## 8. Measurement Framework

**Client signals:** did they pay without heavy negotiation, did they complete the full process without dropping out, did they say the assessment itself felt valuable (asked directly in the debrief), would they refer someone else.

**Methodology signals:** which stages ran smoothly versus which felt awkward or unclear in practice, where an assumption from the Preparation Brief turned out wrong once tested live, where a stage took meaningfully longer than the estimates in section 5.

**Business signals:** whether the client expressed implementation interest unprompted, whether a referral was actually made (not just offered), whether any pilot client represents a plausible repeat or expanded engagement later.

All of this is tracked in `Validation_Dashboard.md`, updated after every pilot stage, not reconstructed from memory at the end.

## 9. Lessons Learned Process

Exactly as defined in `Post_Conversation_Review_Process.md` and `Governance.md` section 4 — nothing new here, restated for clarity in this operational context: every pilot produces a structured reflection (`AI Prescription.md` section 8). A finding that appears in only one pilot is logged in `13 Lessons Learned.md` and watched, not acted on. A finding that repeats across two or more pilots is a candidate for promotion into a named source-of-truth document (methodology, SOP, template, or philosophy), decided at the Phase 1 review point in `10 Roadmap.md`, not mid-pilot. No methodology change happens from a single client's feedback, regardless of how compelling it feels in the moment.

## 10. First 30-Day Pilot Roadmap

**Week 1:** Complete founder preparation checklist (section 6). Begin outreach — aim to have 2-3 qualifying conversations underway by end of week. Book first Assessment if a client is ready.

**Week 2:** Complete first 1-2 Assessments end to end (Intake through Debrief). Run the founder-side reflection after each. Continue outreach for remaining pilot slots in parallel.

**Week 3:** Complete 2nd-3rd Assessments. Update `Validation_Dashboard.md` after each. Watch specifically for any finding repeating from an earlier pilot this month — flag immediately as a two-instance candidate, don't wait for the end of the window.

**Week 4:** Complete remaining Assessments (target: all 5 done by day 30, per `10 Roadmap.md` Phase 1). Conduct the Phase 1 review: read all reflections and Lessons Learned entries together, decide what (if anything) has repeated enough to promote, and make the proceed / adjust-and-rerun / pause decision defined in `10 Roadmap.md` section 7.

Realistic slack: given the 2-3 hour/day working budget alongside the full-time job, this roadmap assumes roughly one Assessment completing every 5-7 days, not all 5 run in parallel — if outreach moves faster than delivery capacity, let prospects wait rather than compress any single Assessment's quality to fit more in.

## Principles Underlying This Playbook

The Assessment is valuable even when the answer is "do nothing." We diagnose before prescribing. Client requests are hypotheses, not instructions. AI prepares; humans decide. We measure the quality of decisions, not the number of implementations sold — the Phase 1 review point should judge this pilot by whether five honest diagnoses were delivered well, not by how many of them turned into build contracts.
