# Website Strategy

> **Layer:** Operations (working document, not source of truth) | **Purpose:** The role of the website in the client journey — strategy only, no copy or slogans | **Status:** Active | **Owner:** Founder | **Version:** 1.1

This expands `Market_Entry_Plan.md` section 2 into full page-level detail (visitor question, trust problem, next action per page) and is the reference to build the actual site from later. No copy, headlines, or slogans exist here — that's a separate, later task, done only once this strategy is settled.

## 1. Website Objective

**What job the website performs:** convince a skeptical, busy business owner that a genuinely honest diagnosis is being offered, and move them toward a real conversation — not a purchase. The website's job ends at "start a conversation" or "book an Assessment"; it is not responsible for closing a sale, explaining every detail, or replacing the founder's own judgement in a live conversation.

**What it should not try to do:** it should not try to sound impressive, comprehensive, or busier than the business actually is. It should not attempt to demonstrate AI expertise through technical depth — the target visitor doesn't want a technical education, they want to feel understood. It should not try to generate volume traffic through broad AI-trend content — that belongs to a different kind of business than the one described in `00 Vision.md` and `Company Philosophy.md`.

## 2. Target Visitor Mindset

**What they're feeling before arriving:** mild but real frustration, usually not urgent-feeling but persistent — the "we've always done it this way" tiredness described throughout `AI Prescription.md`. Some curiosity or pressure about AI generally (from news, peers, or vague FOMO) mixed with genuine skepticism about whether it applies to a business their size. Wariness from having seen generic "AI will transform your business" marketing before and not trusting it.

**What they're unsure about:** whether their specific business (often not tech-forward, often small) is even a sensible candidate for this at all. Whether "AI" means something genuinely useful or just another expensive, complicated system to learn. Whether talking to someone about this commits them to buying something.

**What objections they have:** "we're too small/too old-fashioned for this." "I've heard AI pitches before and they're all hype." "I don't have time to learn a new system." "This will probably just tell me to buy something I don't need." Every page on the site should be able to point to at least one of these objections and show, structurally, why it doesn't apply here.

## 3. Required Pages

Full page-level detail (visitor state of mind, trust barriers, key concepts, evidence required, connection to the AI Prescription journey) is now defined in `Website_Content_Architecture.md`, which supersedes the summaries below as the canonical version. The summaries here remain as a quick-reference overview only.

**Home**
Purpose: establish the core promise (diagnosis before prescription) and route the visitor to the right next page based on where they are in the mindset above.
Visitor question answered: "Is this actually different from every other AI pitch I've seen?"
Trust problem solved: skepticism from prior generic AI marketing exposure.
Desired next action: move to "How It Works" (if still deciding) or "AI Prescription Assessment" (if ready to consider booking).

**AI Prescription Assessment**
Purpose: describe the actual paid offer plainly — what it is, what it costs, what happens, and that the outcome may be "you don't need this," per `AI_Prescription_Assessment_Specification.md`.
Visitor question answered: "What exactly am I paying for, and what happens to me?"
Trust problem solved: fear of being sold something disguised as a diagnosis.
Desired next action: book a conversation or start the process.

**How It Works**
Purpose: plain-language walkthrough of the diagnose-before-prescribe sequence (`AI Prescription.md`'s core sequence, translated for a business owner, not a consultant).
Visitor question answered: "What actually happens, step by step, and is it complicated for me?"
Trust problem solved: fear of an opaque or time-consuming process.
Desired next action: move to "AI Prescription Assessment" or "Who It Helps."

**Who It Helps**
Purpose: describe the target client profile honestly (`ideal_client.md`) so visitors can self-assess fit before ever talking to the founder.
Visitor question answered: "Is this actually meant for a business like mine?"
Trust problem solved: "we're too small/too old-fashioned for this" objection — directly addressed, not just implied.
Desired next action: move to "AI Prescription Assessment" if they recognise themselves, or leave without wasting either party's time if they don't.

**About Founder**
Purpose: establish the founder as a real, credible, relatable person, not a faceless agency — drawing honestly on `about-founder.md`, including the non-technical starting point as a credibility asset, not something to downplay.
Visitor question answered: "Who am I actually going to be dealing with?"
Trust problem solved: distrust of anonymous agencies or oversized claims from a business that's actually one person.
Desired next action: move to "Contact / Book Conversation" once personal trust is established.

**Resources**
Purpose: house the educational content described in `Market_Entry_Plan.md` section 3 — real thinking given away openly, not gated.
Visitor question answered: "Does this person actually know what they're talking about, in a way I can verify before paying?"
Trust problem solved: skepticism that "expertise" claims are just marketing.
Desired next action: return later, or move directly to "AI Prescription Assessment" if a specific article resolves their hesitation.

**Contact / Book Conversation**
Purpose: the lowest-commitment possible next step — a conversation, not a purchase, matching the actual first stage of `Founder_Outreach_System.md`.
Visitor question answered: "What happens if I reach out — am I committing to anything?"
Trust problem solved: fear of an aggressive sales follow-up once contact is made.
Desired next action: an actual real conversation, per section 5 below.

## 4. Positioning Rules

**Phrases to avoid:** "AI-powered," "cutting-edge," "revolutionary," "transform your business," "unlock the power of AI," "future-proof" — all hype language that signals exactly the kind of agency this isn't (`Company Philosophy.md` Principle 6, "no hype ever"). Also avoid falsely humble-sounding but actually vague claims like "we help businesses grow" that could describe literally any service business.

**Claims that must not be made:** no specific promised outcome without evidence to support it (no "save 10 hours a week" claims until real, conservative pilot data supports a range). No implied guarantee that AI will be recommended, or that any engagement leads to a build. No client logos, testimonials, or "trusted by" language before they're genuinely real (per `Market_Entry_Plan.md` section 2's Case Studies guidance).

**How to communicate "no AI needed" outcomes:** stated as a feature of the process, not a disclaimer buried in fine print — the site should say, plainly and prominently, that some assessments conclude AI isn't the right fit, and frame this as proof the diagnosis is real rather than a risk to the visitor. Once real pilot data exists, the actual proportion of "no action" or "no AI" outcomes should be shared honestly, even if it's a meaningful percentage — this number is a trust asset, not something to minimise.

## 5. Conversion Journey

Visitor arrives, usually via a direct link, LinkedIn post, or referral (per `Market_Entry_Plan.md` section 4), already carrying some version of the mindset in section 2.

↓ **Understanding** — Home and/or How It Works resolve "is this actually different," giving the visitor a correct mental model of what's being offered before anything is asked of them.

↓ **Trust** — About Founder and Resources do the work of making the offer feel credible and human, not corporate or hype-driven; Who It Helps lets the visitor self-select rather than being sold to.

↓ **Assessment Interest** — the AI Prescription Assessment page answers the practical questions (cost, process, honest-outcome caveat) once trust and understanding already exist — asked too early, before understanding and trust, this page reads as a sales pitch instead of an honest offer.

↓ **Conversation** — Contact / Book Conversation is the only real conversion goal of the site; everything before it exists to make this step feel safe and low-pressure rather than committing.

The website's only job is to move a visitor down this chain as far as their current mindset allows — it is not expected or designed to convert a first-time, cold visitor straight to a paid booking without passing through understanding and trust first.

## 6. Content Integration

**With founder conversations:** real objections and questions surfaced in actual outreach conversations (`Founder_Outreach_System.md` section 8, `Pilot_Client_Interview_Log.md`) should directly inform what the Resources section covers — the site should answer questions real people have actually asked, not questions a consultant assumes they'd ask.

**With Lessons Learned:** once a pattern is confirmed (not a single observation) per `Post_Conversation_Review_Process.md` and promoted per Governance.md, and if it's relevant to how the business is explained externally, it should be reflected on the site — for example, if the "requests that echo a previously failed fix" pattern becomes well-established, that insight could become genuinely useful public content, written generically enough to protect client confidentiality.

**With future case studies:** the Case Studies/Results area referenced in `Market_Entry_Plan.md` stays empty or clearly marked as upcoming until real, anonymised pilot outcomes exist — at that point it becomes the strongest trust-building content on the site, specifically because it can include honest "no action" outcomes alongside build outcomes, which most competitors' case study pages structurally cannot do.

## 7. Future Scalability

The structure above should not need to be rebuilt if Digital Footprint later adds implementation, training, subscriptions, or digital products — each of these slots in as a natural extension of an existing page rather than requiring a new site architecture:

**Implementation** — becomes a new section reachable from "AI Prescription Assessment" once a client has an existing report recommending it, not a separate top-level sales page competing with the Assessment for attention.

**Training / courses** — fits naturally under "Resources," extended from free educational content into paid educational products, consistent with the "teach as much as build" principle in `00 Vision.md`, without needing a structurally different part of the site.

**Subscriptions** — would attach to "Implementation" as the ongoing-infrastructure offer once a build exists, not as a standalone page with no diagnosis behind it.

**Digital products** — would also live under "Resources," positioned the same way as training: something earned by first demonstrating real expertise through the free content already there.

The one structural rule to preserve as the business grows: nothing new gets added as a top-level, pre-diagnosis sales page. Every future offer should still be reachable only after the visitor has been through some version of the Understanding → Trust sequence in section 5, never bypassing it just because a new product exists.

## Underlying Standard

Every page, and every future addition to the site, should be checkable against the same three lines: diagnose before prescribe, trust before technology, clarity before automation. A page that tries to sell before it has earned understanding and trust doesn't belong on this site, regardless of how normal that pattern is for other AI or automation businesses.
