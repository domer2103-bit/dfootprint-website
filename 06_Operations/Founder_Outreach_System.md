# Founder Outreach System

> **Layer:** Operations (working document, not source of truth) | **Purpose:** How the founder begins real conversations with potential pilot clients, before any Assessment is booked | **Status:** Active | **Owner:** Founder | **Version:** 1.0

This is not marketing copy and it is not a sales script. It exists to protect one thing above all: the diagnosis-first positioning has to be true from the very first message, not just once someone has paid. If outreach sounds like selling, everything built in `AI_Prescription_Client_Experience.md` starts on the wrong foot. The goal of outreach is not a booked Assessment — it's finding a business where a genuinely useful diagnosis is possible, and being honest enough, early enough, that the wrong businesses self-select out before any time is wasted on either side.

## 1. Who to Approach

People matching `ideal_client.md`, encountered through real context, not cold lists — someone you've spoken to before, a business you've noticed as a customer, an introduction from someone in your existing network, a local business you have a genuine reason to know about. Per `10 Roadmap.md` Phase 1, a deliberate mix: some from personal network, most from semi-cold or cold local contact, so the pilot isn't validated only on people predisposed to say yes to you personally.

## 2. How to Identify Good-Fit Businesses

Look for the signals in `ideal_client.md` before ever mentioning Digital Footprint: roughly 5-50 employees, an owner who's visibly still hands-on (you can often tell this from how they talk about the business, not just headcount), some sign of a business that's busy or growing rather than one that's clearly struggling to survive (a struggling business has more urgent problems than an AI diagnosis, and forcing the fit would be dishonest). Notice, don't assume — a good-fit signal is a reason to have a conversation, not a reason to expect a yes.

## 3. How to Start the First Conversation

Start from genuine curiosity about their business, not from your offer. "How's things going with [business]?" or "What's been the busiest/most frustrating part of running [business] lately?" is a real opening, not a setup line — ask it because you actually want to know, and only bring up Digital Footprint if the conversation naturally gets there. If it's a colder contact where there's no existing relationship to start from, be direct instead of falsely casual: say plainly who you are and why you're reaching out, rather than pretending it's a chance encounter.

## 4. What Not to Say

Don't lead with AI. Don't say "I help businesses implement AI" as an opener — it invites a generic "we're not really an AI business" brush-off before the actual value (an honest diagnosis of where they're losing time) has even been described. Don't describe specific Automation Catalog products before any diagnosis has happened — that's prescribing before diagnosing, in outreach form, and it's the exact failure mode the whole methodology exists to prevent. Don't oversell certainty ("I can definitely help you save X hours") — nothing is known yet, and promising an outcome before any evidence exists undermines the honesty the whole positioning depends on. Don't use false urgency or scarcity language ("limited spots," "act now") — the founding-client pricing is real and limited by actual capacity, and should be described that way, not dramatised.

## 5. Discovery Conversation Structure

A short, genuine conversation, not a pitch meeting — usually the informal chat described in section 3, extended naturally if there's real interest:

**Open with genuine interest** in their business and what's been going on, per section 3.

**Listen for a real friction point**, named unprompted — don't ask "do you have any problems AI could solve," ask what's frustrating, slow, or repetitive, in their own words, the same spirit as `Client_Intake_Form.md` Section C.

**Reflect it back honestly**, without immediately proposing a fix: "that sounds like it's costing you real time" is enough — resist the urge to diagnose on the spot, in a casual conversation, with no evidence yet.

**Only then, if it feels natural, introduce what you do** — see section 6.

**Ask if they'd be open to a proper look**, not "would you like to buy an assessment" — "would it be useful if I properly looked into where that time's actually going and whether there's a sensible fix" is an offer to help, not a sales close.

## 6. How to Introduce the Paid Assessment

Plainly, and with the honest-outcome caveat included from the very first mention, not saved for later: "I run a paid diagnosis for businesses like yours — I look properly at where time's being lost and work out what, if anything, is worth fixing with AI. Sometimes the honest answer is that AI isn't the right fix at all, and that's a completely normal result. I'm running a few of these at a reduced rate at the moment while I build out real case studies." This states the offer, the price framing, and the "no AI needed is a valid outcome" principle in one honest breath, matching `AI_Prescription_Client_Experience.md` section 2 moved earlier into the sales conversation itself.

## 7. Handling Objections

**"I don't think we need AI."** Agree that might be exactly right, and say so — "that's a completely valid outcome of the diagnosis itself, and honestly a good number of these end that way. The value is knowing for sure, not being talked into something." This isn't a rebuttal technique, it's simply true, and saying it plainly is more persuasive than any scripted counter would be.

**"How do I know this is worth paying for?"** Be honest about being early — "you'd be one of the first few I'm doing this with, which is why it's a reduced rate. What you get either way is a proper, honest look at where your time's going, in writing, that you keep regardless of what we decide to do next." Don't oversell a track record that doesn't exist yet.

**"Can you just tell me what you'd recommend now?"** Decline honestly — "I genuinely don't know yet, and I wouldn't want to guess and be wrong — that's exactly what the proper look is for." This is a direct, real demonstration of the diagnose-before-prescribe principle, not just a description of it.

**"I'm too busy right now."** Accept it without pushing — "totally understand, the offer's open whenever it's useful" — and note it for a later, respectful check-in rather than repeated follow-up pressure.

## 8. Recording Learning From Conversations

Every real outreach conversation, whether it leads to a booked Assessment or not, gets a short entry using `Client_Conversation_Capture_Template.md` (even an abbreviated version for a short conversation) — what was said, what friction was named, whether they agreed to proceed, and why or why not if they didn't. This feeds `Post_Conversation_Review_Process.md` exactly as any other conversation would: one-off observations are watched, repeated patterns (e.g. a specific objection coming up more than once, or a specific type of business consistently saying no) are logged in `13 Lessons Learned.md` and reviewed at the Phase 1 review point in `10 Roadmap.md`, not acted on from a single conversation.

## The Underlying Standard

If a version of this document ever reads as more persuasive than honest, it's wrong, even if it books more Assessments — the entire value of Digital Footprint depends on the diagnosis being real, starting from the first sentence of outreach, not just from the moment payment is made.
