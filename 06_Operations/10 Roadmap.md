## Phase 1 — Validation

**Purpose:** Test whether the AI Prescription Assessment creates genuine value for real businesses, before scaling anything.

**Validation philosophy:** We are not trying to prove businesses need AI. We are testing whether a diagnosis-first approach helps businesses understand where improvement is possible, and whether AI is appropriate — including when the honest answer is no.

**Principle:** No changes to methodology from single feedback. Repeated patterns create improvements.

### 1. Objective

Complete a small number of real, paid pilot Assessments to find out whether the diagnosis-first approach is genuinely valuable to real business owners — not whether they'll buy an AI build.

### 2. Key Assumptions Being Tested

That business owners will pay real money for a diagnosis-only product with no track record yet. That the 5-50 employee, owner-still-hands-on, no-IT-department profile is the right pilot target. That an honest "you don't need AI" outcome is received as valuable, not as a disappointment. That the SOP and client toolkit hold up in real sessions, not just on paper.

### 3. 30-Day Activities

Recruit and complete 5 pilot Assessments through the existing SOP and toolkit, unmodified. Source a deliberate mix of contacts, not only warm ones. Run a short founder-facing reflection after every pilot, in addition to the client-facing debrief. Full detail on outreach approach, qualifying questions, and conversation framing lives in a separate document — see `Validation Experiment Plan.md` (not yet created).

### 4. Pilot Client Criteria

Roughly matches `ideal_client.md`: 5-50 employees, owner still involved day to day, no IT department, open to being an early/"founding" client at a reduced rate. At least 2 from personal network (acquaintances, not close friends, to reduce false-positive social pressure), at least 3 sourced cold or semi-cold.

### 5. Success Signals

Client pays without heavy negotiation. Client asks about next steps unprompted. Client says they'd refer someone else. Client reacts with relief or genuine interest — not disappointment — when a recommendation is "don't build this."

### 6. Measurement Criteria

Per SOP section 10 reflection, plus: how the client reacted to a "no AI needed" finding, payment friction, whether any SOP or template step felt awkward or slow in practice, actual time taken per stage versus SOP estimates, and whether the pre-qualifying conversation correctly predicted a good-fit client.

### 7. Review Point and Decision Process

Review after all 5 pilots are complete (or 30 days, whichever comes first). Only patterns that repeat at least twice across the 5 pilots are candidates for promotion into AI Prescription.md, SOP, or the client toolkit, via the existing Governance.md promotion path. Single-pilot feedback is logged in `13 Lessons Learned.md` and watched, not acted on. Decision at review: proceed to Phase 2 (broader outreach), adjust and re-run a second small validation round, or pause and reassess the offer entirely.
