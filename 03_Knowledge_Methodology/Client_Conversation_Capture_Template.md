# Client Conversation Capture Template

> **Layer:** Knowledge & Methodology | **Purpose:** Standard structure for recording every real business owner conversation during validation and beyond | **Status:** Active — template, copy per conversation | **Owner:** Founder | **Version:** 1.0

Used for any conversation with a business owner — pre-qualifying calls, full Assessment sessions, informal conversations during outreach. The core discipline of this template is keeping fact and assumption in physically separate sections, so nothing gathered here can quietly drift from "they said" into "we concluded" without a deliberate step in between.

## Conversation Metadata

Date. Business (name or anonymised reference). Who was present (role, not necessarily name). Conversation type (pre-qualifying / Assessment session / informal / other). Duration.

## Section A — Exact Words (Facts)

Direct quotes or as-close-to-verbatim phrases the business owner (or staff member) used to describe their situation, frustrations, or requests. No paraphrasing, no interpretation, no cleanup. If unsure of the exact phrase, mark it "approximate" rather than presenting it as verbatim. This section exists specifically to preserve the client's own language, which matters for two reasons: it protects against the consultant's own bias creeping into the record, and it feeds directly into writing-style.md and future marketing language that should sound like real clients, not like an agency's guess at how clients talk.

## Section B — Observed Facts (Not Interpreted)

What was directly observed or demonstrated — a document shown, a process walked through live, a number stated, a system seen in use. Still facts, not conclusions: "they showed a spreadsheet with 40 rows of overdue invoices" is a fact; "their invoicing is a mess" is already an interpretation and belongs in Section C.

## Section C — Assumptions and Interpretations (Consultant's Read)

Anything inferred, guessed at, or concluded by the consultant, clearly separated from Sections A and B. This section is expected to contain hunches — that's fine, as long as they're labelled as such and never quietly merged with fact. Include confidence level for each (low / medium / high) and what evidence, if gathered later, would confirm or overturn it.

## Section D — Requests Made By the Client

Anything the client specifically asked for, including if they named "AI" or a specific tool. Recorded neutrally here, without judgement on whether it's the right fix — that judgement happens later, in the Evidence Extraction Framework and, if this is a full Assessment, in AI Prescription.md's diagnosis process.

## Section E — Open Questions

Anything left unclear that would need a follow-up conversation or a second perspective (another staff member, a different vantage point) to resolve.

## Consultant Sign-Off

Confirm before filing: is anything in Section A or B actually an interpretation that's crept in from Section C? Re-read once specifically checking for this — it's the most common failure mode of a capture template like this one.
