# Pilot Client Interview Log

> **Layer:** Knowledge & Methodology | **Purpose:** Learning instrument for real pilot conversations — not a sales tracker | **Status:** Active — template, copy per conversation, no data yet | **Owner:** Founder | **Version:** 1.0

This is not `Pilot_Execution_Dashboard.md` (which tracks pipeline status and outreach logistics) and it is not a sales record. It exists purely to capture evidence from real conversations for learning purposes, using the same fact/assumption discipline as `Client_Conversation_Capture_Template.md`, extended with fields specific to validating the pilot itself — how people react to AI, and whether the diagnosis-first approach actually lands. No entries exist yet; this stays a blank instrument until real conversations happen. Nothing here should ever be filled in with invented or hypothetical material.

---

## 1. Conversation Details

Date. Business (name or anonymised reference). Industry. Participant name/role (owner, manager, staff member, etc.). Conversation type (outreach / pre-qualifying / Assessment session / other, per `Pilot_Execution_Dashboard.md` conversation tracking).

## 2. Exact Client Language

Direct phrases, as close to verbatim as possible, with no paraphrasing or cleanup — same discipline as Section A of `Client_Conversation_Capture_Template.md`. Mark anything uncertain as "approximate" rather than presenting it as exact. This is the most valuable section for future writing-style and marketing language, because it preserves how real business owners actually talk, not how a consultant summarises them.

## 3. Business Situation

Team size (stated or estimated). Processes mentioned, in whatever terms the client used. Current frustrations named, if any. Existing tools and systems mentioned.

## 4. Problems Discovered

Kept in three separate, clearly labelled columns or sections — never merged:

**Client-stated problems** — what they said was wrong, in their words.

**Observed facts** — anything directly seen or demonstrated (a document, a number, a system in use). Only what was actually observed, not inferred from what was said.

**Consultant hypotheses** — the consultant's own read, explicitly labelled as a guess, with a confidence level (low/medium/high) and what would need to be true to confirm or overturn it.

## 5. AI Perception

Did they mention AI unprompted, or only when asked? What did they say, in their own words? What assumptions do they seem to hold about what AI can or can't do (recorded as observed, not corrected or debated in the moment)? What concerns, if any, did they raise (cost, trust, job impact, complexity, something else)?

## 6. Trust Signals

What specifically seemed to create interest or engagement during the conversation? What created hesitation, discomfort, or a change in tone? Did the diagnosis-first framing (per `Founder_Outreach_System.md`) get a visible reaction — positive, skeptical, confused, indifferent? Quote the reaction where possible rather than summarising it.

## 7. Lessons

What surprised the consultant in this conversation, compared to what was expected going in? What should be watched for in future conversations, without concluding anything yet? Does this resemble anything from a previous conversation closely enough to flag as a possible repeated pattern — if so, name which prior entry it resembles.

---

## Governance Reminder (Do Not Skip)

A single entry in this log is a one-off observation, per `Post_Conversation_Review_Process.md` — it is watched, not acted on. It does not get written into `13 Lessons Learned.md` as a pattern, and it must never be used to diagnose this or any client from one conversation alone. Only when the same finding appears independently in a second conversation does it become a genuine candidate for a Lessons Learned entry, and only repeated patterns confirmed at a review point are ever promoted into a permanent document, per Governance.md section 4. No principle, methodology change, or new document is created from a single entry in this log, no matter how compelling it seems in the moment.
