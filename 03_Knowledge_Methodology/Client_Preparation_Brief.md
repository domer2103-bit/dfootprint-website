# Client Preparation Brief

> **Layer:** Knowledge & Methodology | **Purpose:** The document that sits between Client_Intake_Form.md and the live Assessment session — prepares the consultant without forming conclusions | **Status:** Active — template, copy per client | **Owner:** Founder | **Version:** 1.0

This is not a diagnosis document. It contains no conclusions, no recommendations, and no AI Suitability Test results — those only exist after the live Assessment session, per AI Prescription.md. Its only job is to make the consultant walk into the room better prepared than a blank page would allow, while keeping "the intake creates a hypothesis, the diagnosis discovers the reality" fully intact. The AI prepares the consultant. It does not replace the consultant, and nothing here is shown to the client.

## 1. Sections of the Brief

**Business Snapshot** — factual summary pulled directly from the intake (size, role, tenure, systems in use). No interpretation.

**Hypotheses Identified** — each friction point raised in the intake, listed individually, in the client's own words where possible, mapped to a Business_Problems.md category where one clearly fits (or flagged as unmatched if not).

**Contradictions and Unknowns** — anything inconsistent across the client's own answers, and anything the intake left genuinely unclear. Kept as its own distinct section, never folded into the narrative summary (see section 5).

**Who to Include** — names or roles suggested for the session, based on which hypotheses involve a process someone other than the person who completed the intake actually performs.

**Suggested Investigation Questions** — per hypothesis, candidate questions modelled on Assessment_Interview_Guide.md, ready for the consultant to review, edit, or discard.

**Consultant Notes** — a blank section for the consultant's own pre-session thinking, entirely separate from anything AI-generated, so the two can never be mistaken for each other.

**Sign-Off** — a required checklist confirming the consultant has reviewed and taken ownership of the brief before the session (see section 4).

## 2. What Comes Directly From the Client

Everything in the Business Snapshot section. All quoted material in Hypotheses Identified — presented as close to verbatim as the intake captured it, following the same discipline as Section A of `Client_Conversation_Capture_Template.md`. Nothing in this category is ever edited for tone or clarity in a way that changes its meaning; if a quote is unclear, it's flagged as unclear, not smoothed over.

## 3. What AI May Generate

Grouping of raw answers into Business_Problems.md categories, with unmatched items flagged rather than forced. Detection of contradictions or inconsistencies across the client's own answers (see section 5). A first draft of investigation questions per hypothesis, following the Assessment_Interview_Guide.md structure (evidence request, involvement test, root-cause probe). A suggested list of who else might be worth including in the session, based on stated roles. Every AI-generated item in the brief is visually or structurally labelled as such — never presented in the same format as client-sourced or consultant-approved material.

## 4. What Requires Consultant Ownership

The final list and order of hypotheses to investigate in the session — the AI may group and suggest, but the consultant decides what actually gets prioritised in limited session time. Every suggested investigation question must be reviewed and either approved, edited, or discarded before the session — none go into the session unreviewed. The final decision on who to invite to the session. Any judgement about whether a flagged contradiction is worth raising directly with the client or worth testing more subtly. The Sign-Off checklist itself, which the consultant must complete before the brief is considered ready to use — confirming they've read every section, not skimmed the summary.

## 5. Displaying Contradictions and Unknowns

Contradictions are shown side by side, quoting both answers directly, with no attempt to resolve or explain them: "Answer to B2 rates daily burden as 4/5. Answer to C3 states that if nothing changes, 'probably nothing catastrophic' would happen." No AI-generated interpretation of what the contradiction means is included in this section — that judgement belongs to the consultant, live, with the client. Unknowns (questions the intake left unclear or didn't cover) are listed as plain, direct open questions, not disguised as assumptions or silently dropped. Every contradiction and unknown must appear somewhere in the Suggested Investigation Questions section, so nothing flagged is ever left untested.

## 6. Generating Suggested Interview Questions

For each hypothesis, three question types are drafted, mirroring Assessment_Interview_Guide.md exactly: a request for real evidence ("what would show me this happening, can I see an example"), a question testing who else is involved and whether their account would match, and a root-cause probe drawn from AI Prescription.md section 2 (is this really the root problem, what happens if nothing changes). Where the intake included a specific client request (Client_Intake_Form.md section F1), at least one generated question must explicitly test that request as a hypothesis, per AI Prescription.md v1.1 — never omitted, and never phrased in a way that assumes the request is correct. Every generated question is labelled "AI-drafted" until the consultant has reviewed it, at which point it's marked "consultant-approved," "consultant-edited," or "discarded" — the brief should always show, at a glance, which questions have actually been vetted by a person and which haven't.

## Explicit Boundary Statement

If this brief ever starts to contain a ranked "most likely problem," a suggested solution, or language that reads like a conclusion rather than a question, that is a design failure and should be corrected immediately — consistent with the same boundary already stated in `AI_Assisted_Intake_System_Design.md`. The brief prepares the conversation. It does not have the conversation, and it does not decide what the conversation will find.
