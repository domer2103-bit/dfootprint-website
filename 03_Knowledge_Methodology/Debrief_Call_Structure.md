# Debrief Call Structure

> **Layer:** Knowledge & Methodology | **Purpose:** Structure for explaining findings and next steps to the client, live | **Status:** Active — template, copy per client | **Owner:** Founder | **Version:** 1.0

A 20–30 minute call, held after the Prescription Report is drafted but sent to the client immediately following this call, not before.

## 1. Reopen the Frame (2 minutes)

Restate the purpose briefly, same as the Assessment session opening: "This is the clarity we promised — some of it may be 'build this,' some of it may be 'don't,' and both are useful outcomes."

## 2. Walk the Roadmap First (5 minutes)

Show the one-page roadmap summary before any individual finding. The client should understand the shape of the whole recommendation — what's first, what's later, what's not happening — before getting into detail on any one item.

## 3. Go Through Each Friction Point (10–15 minutes)

For each: state the finding, the evidence behind it, and the recommendation, in that order. Pause for questions after each one rather than saving all questions to the end. Give particular attention and full explanation to anything recommended against — against AI, or against implementation altogether — since this is the finding most likely to surprise or disappoint a client who came in expecting a build, and it needs to land as a credible, evidence-based call, not a shrug.

## 4. Check Understanding, Not Just Delivery

Ask directly: "Does this match what you were expecting, or is anything here surprising?" Treat a surprised reaction as useful information, not a problem to smooth over — note it for the reflection step afterward (SOP section 10).

## 5. Close Without Selling

State plainly that no further action is required, the report stands on its own, and any implementation is a separate, subsequently-scoped conversation. Do not use this moment to pitch. If the client asks directly about next steps, answer honestly and simply, without pressure.

## 6. Send the Written Report

Immediately after the call, even though everything in it was just discussed live. The client should have both the conversation and the document.

## Consultant Note

If a debrief consistently produces confusion or surprise at the same type of finding, that's a signal for the reflection step in SOP section 10 and possibly Lessons Learned — the report or the interview guide may need adjusting, not just this one client's expectations.
