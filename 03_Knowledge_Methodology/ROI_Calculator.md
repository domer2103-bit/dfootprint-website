# ROI Calculator

> **Layer:** Knowledge & Methodology | **Purpose:** Standard method for calculating and presenting ROI on every automation | **Status:** Active — source of truth | **Owner:** Founder | **Version:** 1.0

Every prescription should end with a number, not a feature list. "This uses AI" means nothing to a business owner. "This saves you 12 hours a month" is something they can act on. This document is the standard method for calculating that number for any automation we build.

## The Core Formula

Time saved per month × hourly value of that time = monthly value delivered.

Monthly value delivered − monthly cost of the solution = net monthly ROI.

## Step 1: Measure the Current Cost of the Problem

Before prescribing anything, find out:

How long does this task currently take, per occurrence? How often does it happen — daily, weekly, monthly? Who does it — the owner, a member of staff, or is it not getting done at all and costing them in missed opportunities?

Multiply frequency by time per occurrence to get total hours per month currently spent.

Example: quotations take 20 minutes each, 40 quotes a month, done by the owner. That's roughly 13 hours a month.

## Step 2: Assign a Value to That Time

Use one of these, in order of preference:

If it's the owner's time, use what an hour of their time is realistically worth to the business (not just a wage figure — factor in what they could be doing instead, like selling or serving customers).

If it's a staff member's time, use their loaded hourly cost (wage plus overhead).

If the task isn't being done at all (e.g. lead follow-up), estimate the value of the opportunity being missed instead of a time value — this becomes a revenue-recovery case rather than a time-saving case.

## Step 3: Calculate Time Saved by the Automation

Estimate realistically, not optimistically. Automations rarely remove 100% of a task — usually they remove the repetitive core and leave a smaller supervisory step.

Time saved = (current time spent) − (time spent after automation, including any review/oversight time).

## Step 4: Calculate the Number

Hours saved per month × value per hour = £ value delivered per month.

Present this alongside the monthly cost of running the solution (hosting, subscriptions, our fee) to show net value, not just gross savings.

## Step 5: Present It Simply

Always lead with the plain-English outcome, not the mechanism.

Good: "This saves you roughly 12 hours a month — about £480 in owner time — for a solution costing £150 a month."

Avoid: "This uses an AI agent connected via webhook to automate quote generation."

## Standard Talking Points

Time saved per month, in hours. Estimated £ value of that time. Cost of the solution. Net monthly value. Payback period if there was a one-off setup cost.

## Notes on Honesty

Never inflate the estimate to make the pitch look better — this breaks the "no hype" principle and damages trust once the client sees the real result. If real usage data becomes available after go-live, revisit the estimate and correct it with the client. Being right after the fact matters more than sounding impressive up front.
