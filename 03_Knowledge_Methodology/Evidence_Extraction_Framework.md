# Evidence Extraction Framework

> **Layer:** Knowledge & Methodology | **Purpose:** How to turn individual conversation captures into repeated problems, root causes, and cross-business patterns | **Status:** Active — source of truth | **Owner:** Founder | **Version:** 1.0

This framework operates on completed Client Conversation Capture Template records. It does not generate new findings on its own — it's the method for reading real evidence once it exists. Nothing in this document should ever be filled in with hypothetical or invented examples; it stays a blank method until real conversations produce real material to run through it.

## Step 1: Identifying Repeated Problems

A "repeated problem" requires the same underlying friction to appear in Section A or B (facts, not assumptions) of at least two separate conversation captures, described independently rather than because one client heard it from another. Track repetition using the categories already defined in `Business_Problems.md` (phone calls, appointment booking, quotations, invoices, lead follow-up, customer support, social media, internal documentation, scheduling, staff onboarding, email overload, review management, reporting, data entry, knowledge management) — if something doesn't fit an existing category, that itself is worth noting rather than forcing a fit.

A single mention, however compelling, is not yet a repeated problem — it's a candidate to watch for next time, logged but not acted on (see Post-Conversation Review Process).

## Step 2: Distinguishing Symptoms From Root Causes

For any friction point being examined, apply the same root-cause questions already established in AI Prescription.md section 2: is this really the root problem, what evidence supports that, what assumptions are being made. A symptom is what the client notices and names ("we keep missing calls"); a root cause is what's actually generating it, and it can only be confirmed with Section B evidence (observed facts), never Section A alone (their own words describing the symptom).

A practical check: if fixing the named problem directly wouldn't actually resolve the client's underlying complaint, it was a symptom, not a root cause — the stress test cases already produced real examples of this (quoting speed being blamed on admin capacity when the real cause was inconsistent pricing).

## Step 3: Identifying Patterns Across Businesses

Only look for cross-business patterns once at least three to five real conversation captures exist — attempting pattern recognition on one or two data points produces false confidence, not insight. When patterns are examined, separate them into three types: patterns in the problem itself (the same friction showing up in different businesses), patterns in the root cause (different-sounding complaints that trace back to the same underlying issue), and patterns in language (the same phrases or framing multiple business owners independently use to describe their frustration — valuable for both diagnosis and future writing-style.md/marketing language).

Any pattern claim must cite which specific conversation captures it's drawn from, by reference, so it can be checked and isn't just a general impression.

## What This Framework Explicitly Does Not Do

It does not decide whether AI is the right answer to a repeated problem — that's AI Prescription.md's job, applied per business, per friction point, not a shortcut derived from cross-business patterns. A repeated problem across businesses is evidence worth investigating with the same rigour each time, not a reason to skip the diagnosis on the assumption that "we've seen this before."
