# Assessment Interview Guide

> **Layer:** Knowledge & Methodology | **Purpose:** The consultant's working guide for the live diagnosis session — helps discover reality, not confirm the hypothesis | **Status:** Active — template, copy per client | **Owner:** Founder | **Version:** 1.0

Used live, during the Assessment session (SOP section 3). This is a guide, not a script to read verbatim — the job is to investigate, not to move through questions mechanically. For the underlying method — mindset, technique, how to challenge respectfully, how to verify with multiple people, and when there's enough evidence to stop — see `Assessment_Interview_Method.md`. This document is the tactical question reference; that one is the craft behind using it well.

## Opening (say this every time, even to repeat-feeling clients)

"This is a diagnosis, not a sales conversation. It's entirely possible we conclude you don't need AI, or don't need us — and that's a genuinely good outcome if it's the honest one."

## Per-Hypothesis Investigation

Repeat this block for every hypothesis raised in the Client Intake Form, and for anything new raised during Open Discovery below.

**State the hypothesis back to them:** "You mentioned [X] — walk me through what actually happens, step by step."

**Ask for evidence, not description:** "Can you show me an example — a real one, not a typical one?" A sample quote, a real inbox, a live screen-share of the system in question.

**Test who's actually involved:** "Who else touches this process? Would they describe it the same way you just did?" If that person is in the room, ask them directly and compare answers.

**Apply the root-cause questions (AI Prescription.md section 2):** Is this really the root problem? What evidence supports that? What assumptions are we making right now? What happens if nothing changes at all?

**If this was a specific request (e.g. "I want an AI for X"):** "Why do you think that's the right fix? What are you actually hoping changes as a result?" Do not accept the request as the brief — dig for the underlying problem it's meant to solve, and test that instead.

**Record, don't conclude:** Log the finding using the six-field template from SOP section 4 (hypothesis, what was found, evidence, who provided it and their role, contradictions between people, and initial notes). Do not state a recommendation or reaction in the room.

## Open Discovery (after all listed hypotheses are covered)

"Is there anything we haven't talked about yet that's actually more frustrating than what's on this list?" Treat anything raised here with the same rigor as a pre-listed item — walk through the same investigation block above.

## Closing

Confirm timeline for the report and debrief. Do not hint at conclusions, reassure, or offer a preview of what the recommendation might be — the analysis happens afterward, deliberately, away from the pressure of the room.

## Consultant Self-Check Before Ending the Session

Has every hypothesis been tested with real evidence, not just discussed? Has more than one perspective been captured wherever more than one person was available? Has every specific client request been treated as a hypothesis, not accepted at face value? Is there anything still unclear that needs a follow-up question before the client leaves, rather than chasing it later by email?
