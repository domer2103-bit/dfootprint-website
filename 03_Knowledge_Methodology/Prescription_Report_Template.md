# Prescription Report Template

> **Layer:** Knowledge & Methodology | **Purpose:** The client-facing document that delivers the diagnosis and recommendations | **Status:** Active — template, copy per client | **Owner:** Founder | **Version:** 1.0

Written in plain language, per writing-style.md — short paragraphs, no jargon, UK English, minimal formatting. This is the deliverable the client keeps regardless of what they decide to do next.

---

## AI Prescription Report — [Client Business Name]

Prepared by Digital Footprint. [Date]

### What We Looked At

A short, plain paragraph: how many friction points were investigated, who was involved in the diagnosis (roles, not names if preferred), and what evidence the findings below are based on.

### The Roadmap (One-Page Summary)

A single, plain-language view of every friction point together, showing what's recommended first, what's recommended later, and what's recommended not to do at all — so the client can see the shape of the whole picture before the detail.

### Findings, One Per Friction Point

Repeat this block for every friction point identified, in priority order. Every friction point gets one, with no silent drops.

**[Friction point name]**

What we found: plain description of what was actually happening, based on evidence gathered, not just what was originally assumed.

Evidence: what was shown or demonstrated that supports this finding.

Recommendation: implement with Digital Footprint / you implement this yourselves / a process fix, not AI / referral to another specialist / monitor and revisit later. Stated plainly and with the same confidence whether the answer is yes or no.

If AI is recommended: which product, roughly what it would cost, roughly what it would save (from ROI_Calculator.md, conservative estimate), and where it sits in the sequence.

If AI is not recommended: what to do instead, and why this is the right call, without hedging.

### What Success Would Look Like

For anything recommended for action, a short note on how it will be judged, referencing all three levels from AI Prescription.md section 7: the operational change expected (time, cost, errors, speed, consistency), what it should free up for the people involved, and what it should mean for the business's underlying capability (less dependency on one person, clearer process, easier to scale).

### What Happens Next

A plain statement that no further action is required, that this report stands on its own, and that implementation — if wanted — is a separate conversation, scoped and priced on its own terms.
